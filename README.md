The Driver Pass project was for Liam, the owner of Driver Pass, and Ian, the IT officer. They wanted a web-based driving training system where customers could register for packages, access online classes and practice tests, schedule driving lessons, and manage their accounts. The system also needed to help employees manage customers, instructors, vehicles, and reservations while maintaining security and tracking account and reservation changes. I think I did particularly well at taking the client’s requirements from the interview and turning them into system designs that represented how Driver Pass would actually operate. If I could revise one part, I would improve my UML diagrams by making the relationships and processes even clearer and ensuring every client requirement was represented.

I interpreted the user’s needs by focusing on the specific problems Driver Pass wanted the system to solve, such as scheduling lessons while matching customers with available instructors and vehicles and providing different levels of access for customers and employees. Considering user needs is important because a system can work technically but still fail if it does not solve the users’ actual problems. When designing software, I would first gather and organize requirements, identify the different users and their responsibilities, and then use tools such as use case, activity, and class diagrams to model the system. I would also get feedback from stakeholders throughout the design process and make adjustments before development. This helps ensure the final system is both functional and useful to the people who will use it.
